# Joint one-lag covariance geometry
Take the normalized positive equal-time spectrum p(k) proportional to the Xi theta density on [0, infinity), k in the units of its Fourier transform. Use spatially even and time-even, real stationary covariances, with exactly that fixed spatial marginal. At a single fixed nonzero time lag tau, any measurable q(k) in [-1,1] is admissible. One constructive realization puts conditional mass (1+q(k))/2 at temporal frequency 0 and splits the rest symmetrically between +/-pi/|tau|. The two-radius correlation profile is the integral of q(k) times (cos(8k),cos(16k)) against p. Maximizing a linear contrast uses q=sign(u1 cos(8k)+u2 cos(16k)). Sign choices at isolated zeros have no effect. The admissible region is convex and centrally symmetric, hence its whole boundary is determined by the support function. This construction addresses one chosen time lag. Arbitrarily prescribing q independently at every time lag is not justified.

## Excluded pair
The independent coordinate supports are 0.635963501981937955 and 0.636619776706673057. Both admit 1/2. The exact joint maximum of the sum is numerically enclosed at 0.929455431236495185..., leaving a gap 0.070544568763504815... for the pair (1/2,1/2). All values are dimensionless. Rounding the individual maxima to 0.636, 0.637 is safe as an illustration but not a rigorous inequality. Printed analytic integration balls are in tight-supports.json.

The reflected-observer equal-time variables Y_j=(X(ja,0)+X(-ja,0))/2, j=1,2, have Gram G_ij=[A((i-j)a)+A((i+j)a)]/2. With X(0,tau) of unit variance, their finite covariance requires c^T G^-1 c <=1. The excluded pair yields 0.8284781471 and the local sum bound sqrt(ones^T G ones)=1.0986506251. Even a two-time five-site finite block remains positive definite for this pair. Use spatial positions 0,8,16,24,32 and times 0,tau; K_ij=A(8(i-j)) and J_ij=b_|i-j| with b=(-3/50,1/2,1/2,0,-9/25). The block [K,J; J,K] has smallest eigenvalue 0.02466248633; the orthogonal time sum/difference gives K+J and K-J. Both K+J-I/50 and K-J-I/50 are positive definite by rigorously positive leading minors. The finite matrix merely supplies a local consistency witness; it cannot be a restriction of an extension with the required full spatial marginal.

## Reproduce
1. python geometry.py generates joint-results.json and joint-boundary.csv with one degree increments of the boundary normal, the two coordinates attaining each normal, and normalized support. This uses 48- or 64-point split Gauss-Legendre quadrature and an independent adaptive SciPy quadrature on selected directions.
2. python certify_geometry.py (python-flint 0.9.0) gives a deliberately coarse, outward-rounded Riemann enclosure and 10x10 local PSD certificate.
3. python tight_support.py (python-flint 0.9.0) rigorously integrates sign-separated entire integrands on [0,3] at 168 bits. Axis cuts occur at odd multiples of pi/(2w). The sum uses cos(8k)+cos(16k)=2cos(12k)cos(4k), changing sign when 8k=2pi m +/-pi/3. At 8k=(2m+1)pi there is a double zero and the sign stays the same.
4. python figure.py regenerates the figure in manuscript/joint-region.png. The ellipse is the reflected-observer bound, not the boundary of all five-site completions. Area and plotting smoothness are illustrative. Samples and arithmetically enclosed results are supplied.
5. numpy 2.3.5; scipy 1.17.0; python-flint 0.9.0; matplotlib required only for plotting.

## Error bound for spectral truncation
A raw term is exp(k/2-X) X(2X-3), X=pi n² exp(2k). This is positive. Substituting X, its integrated tail from k=T is at most (pi n²)^(-1/4) ∫_{x0}^∞ X^(5/4) exp(-X) dX <= (x0²+2x0+2)exp(-x0), x0=pi n²exp(2T)>=1. For x0>=12 the last bound is <=exp(-x0/2), since the exponential wins already at 12 and exp(x/2)/(x²+2x+2) is increasing above 12. For the Riemann proof retain n<=4 on [0,2]; discarded mass < exp(-37.5)/(1-exp(-16.5))+4exp(-1.5 exp(4)) < 1e-15. For the tight proof retain n<=8 on [0,3]; discarded mass < exp(-121.5)/(1-exp(-28.5))+8exp(-1.5 exp(6)) < 1e-51. The normalized axis and sum certificates include omitted mass times coefficient bounds 1 and 2, divided by the enclosed full normalizer. The high precision result is supported by the independent coarse interval and two floating comparisons. There is no fitted physical parameter or observational detection in this calculation.

## Two controls for specificity
For an arbitrary normalized absolutely continuous spectrum, every nonconstant Fourier coefficient at integer multiples of a vanishes as a tends to infinity (Riemann–Lebesgue). Approximation by trigonometric polynomials gives a uniform-phase limit for every continuous periodic function of ak. Hence the axis limits are 2/pi and the sum limit is 3 sqrt(3)/(2 pi): integrate cos x + cos 2x over -pi/3 < x < pi/3, where it is positive, and double the integral of the positive part because its mean is zero. The witness therefore separates for every such spectrum at sufficiently large radii. Also the three-site equal-time Gram tends to I. With cross-time 3-site equicorrelation row (-1/4,1/2,1/2) the eigenvalues of the cross block are 3/4 and -3/4 (twice); the finite two-time covariance passes in the large-radius limit. The qualitative separation does not diagnose arithmetic.

At the same finite a=8, use the matched half-normal spectrum with variance m2=−A''(0). Run python gaussian_control.py with python-flint 0.9.0 to obtain gaussian-comparison.json. Its sum support 0.939730270188668368 exceeds Xi 0.929455431236495185 by 0.01027483895. Its difference support 0.714689135936545559 falls below Xi 0.724283345491981975 by 0.00959420956. Neither admissible region contains the other. Each contrast is attained by choosing the sign of its corresponding mode combination, so there are exact modeled counterexamples in both directions. In particular the Gaussian sum-maximizer has coordinates (0.539672722860055890, 0.400057547328612478). These are comparisons of mathematical spectra; the discrepancy is not an observational error bar or a mechanism claim.
